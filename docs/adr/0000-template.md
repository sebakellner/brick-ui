# NNNN. Decision title in the imperative, one line

Date: YYYY-MM-DD

## Status

One of: proposed, accepted, rejected, deprecated, superseded by
[NNNN](NNNN-slug.md), or superseded in part by [NNNN](NNNN-slug.md) — the last
when only part of the decision is reversed, in which case one sentence under the
status names what still holds.

## Context

The situation that forces the decision. What hurt before and what it cost.

Concrete facts, not adjectives: the file that carried the problem, the error
that was observed, the number of places that had to stay in sync. An ADR is
read years later by someone deciding whether to reverse it, so the context has
to stand on its own without the conversation that produced it.

## Decision

The decision, in the present tense, in one or two sentences. Then the detail
that makes it implementable.

### Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why it was rejected |
| --- | --- |

List the options that were genuinely on the table. An option nobody argued for
is not an alternative, and an ADR that claims otherwise is worse than one with
a single row.

## Consequences

What becomes easier. What becomes harder. What debt is accepted in exchange,
and under which condition it gets revisited.

Both directions are required: a decision with only upsides was not a decision,
it was a preference. Name the most likely mistake the new arrangement invites —
that paragraph is what a future reader needs most.
